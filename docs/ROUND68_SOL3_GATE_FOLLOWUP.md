# Round 68 sol3 visual gate follow-up

This follow-up starts at master `222a0b7` and addresses the four observations in
`/tmp/gate-sol3-b/`.

Husk sleeves now limit intermediate terrain lift to 45% of their radius, fading
that lift to zero at each original grounded joint. Their maximum radii are
0.40/0.30/0.22 m, with tapered sections, and their pigment matches the shell
panels. An alternate reference axis keeps near-vertical segments finite. This
prevents a steep terrain sample from stretching a sleeve into a dark slab.
Two spans retain the ten-sided section and terminal caps while reducing sleeve
triangles from 100 to 60; the full husk vertex count falls from 304,485 to 291,525.

Temple stairs retain the existing walking colliders and bevelled geometry. A
broad gradient below each nose, a gradual normal transition onto the tread, and
small per-tread pigment variation restore step definition. These gradients are
vertex shading, with no fine repeated mortar texture.

The three tiny lawn crowns are removed after scatter arguments consume their
original random draws. The fern replacement also collapsed into flat stars in
the gate view; ordinary lit meadow now fills those roots. Other shrub placement
and pigment remain unchanged.

The authored waterfall receiving-channel material now feathers its rock contact
using a signed bank distance baked into the existing water mesh's spare ledge
component. The distance uses the local bed depth and cross-channel slope; dry
banks are negative. A derivative-aware feather fades actual dry banks; shallow
pigment and broken low-strength foam soften the authored flow perimeter beside
the rock skin.
Retaining pool coverage through that wider pigment transition avoids exposing a
dark hidden-bed stripe. The falling sheet uses the established plunge envelope.
No new per-frame geometry, draw, varying, or texture lookup is added.
